I am a Senior Research Scientist at Woven by Toyota, focusing on the application of machine learning in autonomous driving. Before, I worked at the Toyota Research Institute. Projects

New · 2026

Goal Revisions and Support Failures in Contact-Rich Whole-Body Skill Composition

A simulated humanoid learns arm balances, inversions and single-leg balances from human motion capture. Switching to compatible goal revisions while distilling a whole-body controller substantially improves contact-rich skill composition, but late revisions and unloaded requested supports remain open problems.

Sim-to-Real Transfer for Robots

Developed reinforcement learning based algorithms that enable learning bipedal and quadrupedal walking controllers.

Learning Safe-Falling Strategies for Humanoids

We develop an off-policy reinforcement learning algorithm with a mixture of actor-critic experts to teach robots how to fall by minimizing impulse.

Paper

Improving Humanoid Safety by Combining Model-Based Control with Reinforcement Learning

We develop an algorithm to teach robots to balance without falling when an external push is applied. We combine model-based control inputs with model-free policy learning to improve performance, and present a curriculum that enables efficient learning.

Paper

Joint Space Control

We develop a curriculum learning algorithm to learn joint space control of robot manipulators that achieves low end-effector error (<1 cm), generates smooth control comparable to operational space control, and avoids obstacles.

Paper

Learning to Prevent Falls with an Assistive Device

We develop a reinforcement learning algorithm to learn a fall-prevention control policy for an assistive device such as a lower-limb exoskeleton.

Paper

Visuo-Tactile Control Policy for a Multi-Fingered Robot Hand

We investigated the effect of incorporating different sensing modalities, such as touch and vision, on policy performance in tasks such as grasping on a real-world Kuka-Allegro robot system.

Paper

Learning to Walk on a Treadmill

We learn a control policy to create human agents that can walk on a treadmill in simulation. The biomechanical gait characteristics of the human agent are similar to real-world human walking.
